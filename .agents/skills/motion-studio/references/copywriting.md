# Copywriting for motion

## Structures that work (adapt, don't copy)
- **30s dare-pain-turn-proof-close:** hook (attention dare) → pain named concretely → bold turn with a joke → "So what if..." benefits → proof → velvet rope → brand close.
- **15s:** hook → one promise → one proof → CTA.
- **Word-beat film (R01, R20):** 3-5 single words, each its own full-bleed beat ("Fragmented. Structured. Credible."), then the logo.
- **Product walk (R04, R17):** Introducing → what you need → pointer demo → CTA button.

## Voice
Confident, concrete, a little cheeky; never insulting. Aim provocation at the *situation* (scattered vendors, messy spreadsheets) not the viewer. Humour comes from understatement ("Okay, maybe keep your accountant").
Regulated sectors (finance, health, legal): calm authority, no humour at the client's expense, no guarantees. Prefer verbs of clarity: *see, know, decide, close*.

## Rules
1. **No unbackable promises.** Use "what if" framing and verbs like *help, show, turn*. Never "guaranteed", "page one", "double your revenue" unless the client can prove it.
2. **No invented numbers.** Stats must come from the client. Placeholders are marked `[CLIENT TO CONFIRM]`.
3. **Vary sentence shape.** Six lines of "Thing that does X" is a menu.
4. **One idea per beat.** If a beat needs two sentences on screen, split it.
5. **Taglines on screen, not in the voice** unless they are short and concrete.
6. **Screen and voice don't compete.** Short anchor words on screen; the voice carries the sentence.
7. **Name services in the client's own words**; combine paired services (e.g. social media management + ads = "Social & ads").
8. **Check pronunciation and legal wording** with the client; list every claim in the hand-off.

## Quick budgets
30s ≈ 70-80 spoken words · 15s ≈ 35-40 · each service line ≤ 7 words · on-screen headline ≤ 6 words · hook ≤ 12 words.
