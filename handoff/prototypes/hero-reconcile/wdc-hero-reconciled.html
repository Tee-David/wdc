Homepage hero · proposal 2

Keep the full screen. Put the motion inside it.

Why the last prototype felt off

  1. It left the navy band. Every WDC landing page opens on a dark hero, and the prototype opened on paper, so it read as another site.
  2. The motion sat in a framed card beside the copy. A card is a product demo; the live hero is a place you stand in.
  3. On a phone the card pushed the stage below the buttons, so the immersive first screen the owner likes was gone.
  4. It swapped the headline for new copy, so the voice changed along with the layout.

What the best heroes do

    Measured

    Contrast, every pair

    Text needs 4.5:1 against its own ground; a control's edge and the focus ring need 3:1 against what they sit on. Flat pairs are computed here from the hex values.     Recommendation

    Build C, and let the phone stay full screen

    The call

    One navy ground, edge to edge. The words and the motion share it and never overlap.

    C keeps what the owner likes about the live hero: on a phone the first screen is all band, the motion runs under the header to the edges, and the headline and both buttons are on the first screen of a 390 × 844 phone. It keeps what was liked about the prototype: the ten pieces play whole, uncropped by a scrim, and the service chips turn the hero into a way into the work.

    It fixes the feeling that was off, because nothing is boxed and nothing is on paper. The stage is the band itself, feathered into the copy side, so it reads as one place.

    A is the runner-up: it is the closest to today, but the headline covers the middle of every piece, and the contrast behind the words depends on what the piece is doing that second. B is the most cinematic, and worth adding later as an enhancement of C on desktop only, once C is live and measured.

    LCP

    Today the LCP element is the first hero photograph. In all three concepts the photograph goes, so the LCP becomes the first headline line, which is server-rendered text that never changes. The band's first paint is a CSS radial gradient identical to the pieces' own ground, so the stage fades in from the colour already on screen with no layout shift and no image request. Load the engine after load and each piece on demand (about 10 to 22 KB each, uncompressed, in this prototype).

    Phone performance

    One stage, one piece rendering at a time, canvas capped at 1.5× device pixels on phones. C's stage is about half the screen, so it draws roughly half the pixels of A or B. Everything stops when the hero leaves the screen or the tab is hidden. The WebGL piece (One Object) should be desktop-only, or replaced by its still on low-memory phones. No scroll listener in A or C; B's is passive and coalesced into one frame.

    Accessibility

    Every word in C sits on flat navy, so every ratio in the table is fixed, not a hope. The pause control is a real button, 44px, on screen at all times (WCAG 2.2.2). Under reduced motion each piece shows its designed still, the headline does not type, the tools rail stops, and the chips still switch the still. Chips and buttons are 44px or taller.

    Trade-offs of C

    Less text over image than today, which is the drama some people love in the current hero. On a short phone the copy block is tight: at 390 × 667 it still fits by arithmetic, and at 320 × 568 the chips will fall below the fold, so both need a real test before build. Two pieces (Prism and Signature) both stand for branding, so the chip shows one and the reel alternates.

    Prototype only. Nothing here changes the site. The ten pieces are the ones from the Hero Motion artifact, unchanged, rendered on the band's navy ground in both themes.
