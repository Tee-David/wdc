    Layout A: The Reel

    Time-led. One stage, ten openings, cut together.

    How it works

    The copy sits still on the left. The stage on the right plays each piece's full opening (about ten seconds), holds its idle for a beat, then hands off through an orange iris: the full stop from the WDC. mark opens into the next piece on the same ground, so the cut never flashes.

    Under the stage, a ten-part progress rail names the service now playing and lets people jump to any piece. The pause button sits on the stage.

    On a phone

    The stage becomes a full-bleed band under the header, like a video hero, with the progress bars laid over its foot and 44px previous and next buttons beside the service name. The copy and CTAs follow directly.

    Why: it is the most cinematic of the three and a band is how people read a reel. The cost is height: at 844px the CTAs are on the first screen, but on a 667px phone they sit just below it.

    Performance

    Only the piece on screen renders; the outgoing one is a frozen canvas for the 0.9s hand-off, then it is dropped. It all stops when the hero scrolls away or the tab is hidden. The H1 and CTAs are plain HTML and paint before any motion code runs. In production, load the engine after first paint and each piece on demand.

    What you are choosing

    • Best for: a first visit that should feel like a showreel.
    • Trade-off: a visitor sees one service at a time, in our order, and a full cycle takes about two minutes.
    • Reduced motion: no auto-advance; each piece shows a designed still and the rail still switches between them.
    • Other H1 options: "Creative work people remember." / "We make brands, sites and apps people fall for."

      Layout B: Service-linked

      Intent-led. The left side drives the right.

      How it works

      Under the CTAs sits a quiet list of the seven services. Hover or focus one and the stage wipes across to that service's piece, starting from a lively moment rather than a blank opening. Branding and Social and content each own two pieces and alternate between them.

      At rest the stage shows One Object, the form that stands for every service at once. Left alone, it steps through the list by itself about every eight seconds and marks the one playing.

      On a phone

      There is no hover on a phone, so the list becomes a row of tabs directly above the stage: a native sideways scroll with 44px tabs, so the finger and the thing it changes sit together. The copy and CTAs come first.

      Why: it keeps the services one tap from the CTAs, and the stage never pushes them down.

      Performance

      Hover waits 140ms before switching, so sweeping the pointer down the list does not start seven pieces in a row. Only the target piece runs. The frozen outgoing frame slides under the wipe, which moves by transform alone. Everything pauses off screen and when the tab is hidden.

      What you are choosing

      • Best for: explaining what we do. The hero doubles as a services menu.
      • Trade-off: the busiest left column of the three, and the pieces are seen mid-flight more than from the start.
      • Reduced motion: stills only; choosing a service swaps the still with no wipe.
      • Other H1 options: "One studio for the whole brand." / "From the first mark to the last pixel."

      Layout C: The Strip

      Space-led. All ten laid side by side; the one in front is alive.

      How it works

      The right side is a strip of ten cards that runs off the edge of the screen, so the next piece always shows at the edge. The card in front plays; the others hold a designed still of the same frame, so a card comes alive without a jump. Every nine seconds or so the strip glides to the next card. Ten number buttons and the arrows jump anywhere.

      On a phone

      The same strip becomes a native swipe row under the copy: 80% cards with the next one peeking, CSS scroll-snap, no JavaScript on the gesture. Vertical swipes still scroll the page. It is the one people already know from app stores and stories.

      Performance

      One piece renders at a time. The stills are drawn once, one per idle moment, after the hero has painted. In production they are static images made by a committed script. Which card is in front comes from an IntersectionObserver, not from reading the scroll position on every frame.

      What you are choosing

      • Best for: showing breadth at a glance, and the calmest of the three to live with.
      • Trade-off: the pieces play their idle loops, not their full openings, and it is the least cinematic.
      • Reduced motion: no auto-glide; every card is a still and swiping still works.
      • Other H1 options: "Ten crafts, one studio." / "Creativity you can see working."
